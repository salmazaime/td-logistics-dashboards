# Reflections TD5

## Reflection 1
I think the operations manager will disagree the most. Opening a warehouse brings rent and setup costs that his dashboard does not show, while the CO2 and network views only see shorter distances.

## Reflection 2
I would struggle most to defend the cost figures, 0.12 EUR per tonne per km and 40 EUR per tonne per year. They have no source at all, whereas the CO2 factor sits inside a published range. The storage figure is the same for every warehouse, so a client could just say it is wrong and a lot of my results would fall apart.

## Reflection 3
Lille is the clearest case. On the CO2 and cost pages it looks perfect, with 0 kg of CO2 and no transport cost, so those views see nothing to fix and could even suggest closing it. The network page says the opposite, because closing Lille raises the average distance from 168.04 km to 174.43 km.

## Reflection 4
My top route is Marseille from Lyon, at 277.6 km and about 1.2 million kg of CO2 a year. I would check it before recommending it, because the tonnage is only a guess based on population and Marseille is also a candidate warehouse. If it opens, this route disappears. I would still keep it in mind for a move to rail.

## Reflection 5
Lyon costs the most, about 13.5 million EUR, with 5.7 million in transport and 7.8 million in storage. I would work on transport first. Storage costs the same per tonne everywhere, but Lyon pays 69.21 EUR per tonne against 50.91 for Paris and 40 for Lille, and that gap can only come from long routes.

## Reflection 6
I would open Marseille. It brings the average distance down from 168.04 km to 115.03 km, which beats Bordeaux at 139.89 km, and closing Lille makes it worse. The caveat lowers my confidence in the size of the gain, since a lot of it is just Marseille's own big population no longer being far away. I would still make the same call and check capex and lease costs next. For Reflection 1, I was partly right, because the operations side would be the one to slow things down, but the CO2 numbers also point to Marseille.

## Reflection 7
I added a cost per tonne bar chart to the cost dashboard. The stacked bar mostly shows volume, so it cannot tell me which warehouse is least efficient. Dividing by the tonnes handled answers that, and with one number to compare across three warehouses a simple bar is the clearest choice.
