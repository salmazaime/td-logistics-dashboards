# Reflections TD5

## Reflection 1
I predict the logistics and operations manager will disagree most. Opening a warehouse adds rent and set up costs that none of the dashboards show, while the sustainability and network views only see shorter distances, so they will both say yes to a new warehouse and he will want to wait.

## Reflection 2
I would be least comfortable defending the cost figures, 0.12 EUR per tonne-km and 40 EUR per tonne per year. They have no source at all, while the CO2 factor at least sits inside a published range of about 0.047 to 0.12. The 40 EUR storage figure is the same for every warehouse, so it decides how much storage weighs in the results and a client could simply say it is wrong.

## Reflection 3
Lille is the example. In the CO2 and cost views it looks perfect, with 0 kg of CO2 and 0 EUR of transport cost, so those views see nothing to fix there and a cost cutter might even think about closing it. The network view says the opposite: closing Lille makes the average distance worse, from 168.04 km to 174.43 km, because Lille serves its own 233,098 people.

## Reflection 4
The top route is Marseille from Lyon, at 277.6 km and about 1,212 tonnes of CO2 a year. I would double check it before recommending it, for two reasons. The tonnage is only a population estimate, and Marseille is also one of the candidate warehouses, so if it opens this route simply disappears. I would still keep it as a good rail shift candidate, since Lyon to Marseille is a major rail corridor.

## Reflection 5
Lyon has the highest total cost, about 13.5 million EUR, made of 5.7 million for transport and 7.8 million for storage. I would pull transport first. Storage costs the same 40 EUR per tonne everywhere, so it only grows with volume, but Lyon pays 69.21 EUR per tonne against 50.91 for Paris and 40 for Lille, and that extra can only come from long routes.

## Reflection 6
I would open Marseille. It cuts the population weighted average distance from 168.04 km to 115.03 km, better than Bordeaux at 139.89 km, and closing Lille makes things worse at 174.43 km. The caveat lowers my confidence in the size of the gain, since part of it is just Marseille's own 873,076 people no longer being 278 km away, but it does not change my choice. I would make the same call and flag capex and lease costs as the next step. On Reflection 1, I was partly right: the operations side is the one that would slow things down, though the CO2 numbers also favour opening Marseille.

## Reflection 7
I extended the cost dashboard with a bar chart of cost per tonne for each warehouse. The stacked bar above it is mostly driven by volume, so it cannot tell me which warehouse is actually the least efficient. Dividing by tonnes handled answers that, and with one metric and three categories to compare, a simple bar is clearer than a pie, a line or another stacked chart.
